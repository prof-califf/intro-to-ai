The Decide Project

One real AI system from your own life, taken apart with the tools from this course, tested with a method from the labs, and decided on: keep it, fix it, or drop it, and what that costs.

10 percent of your grade · three pages · due Wed, Dec 2, by 2:00 p.m. · two-minute show-and-tell in class that day

In one paragraph

Pick one AI system that is actually in your life or your job. Run the five critical AI questions on it, using the five parts to answer the first one. Test it with one audit method from the labs. Then decide: keep it, fix it, or drop it, with what it costs and who pays. Write it up in three pages, submit it on Canvas by 2:00 p.m. on Wednesday, December 2, when that day's class starts, and explain it in two minutes during that class, the last class of the quarter. You are graded on whether your decision survives the questions, not on which decision you make. Dropping a system, argued well, earns full credit.

1

Pick a system

Choose something you or people around you actually use, specific enough to name. The scheduling tool at your job, the software that screened your rental application, the chatbot your bank uses, the feed you can't put down, the app that recommends your music, the AI tool you use for one particular class. Specific beats big: "Spotify's Discover Weekly playlist" works; "AI in music" doesn't.

Is it actually AI? Use Chapter 1's test. An AI system learns patterns from data and then makes a judgment (fraud or not, hire or not, this song next) or a creation (a paragraph, an image). If a person wrote every rule by hand and nothing was learned, it isn't AI for this project. If you're not sure, name the kind: predictive, generative, or embodied and agentic. If you can't name one, pick something else.

Works wellNeeds narrowingDoesn't work
A named app or feature you use, at a named company"ChatGPT" (narrow it: ChatGPT as you use it for one class)"AI in general" or "AI and society"
A system that made a decision about you (a screening, a score, a flag)"Social media" (narrow it: one app's feed)A tool with only hand-written rules, like a basic calculator or a fixed thermostat schedule
A tool your workplace or a business you know uses"Self-driving cars" (narrow it: one company's service)A system you can't find any information about at all
2

Choose an audit method

An audit means you test the system, not just describe it. Take one method you practiced in a lab and use it on your own system. You don't need special access; each method below works from the outside. Other lab methods are fine too; check with me first.

MethodFromWhat you doFor example
Two-input test (the two-résumé test)Lab 6Give the system two inputs that are identical except for one detail, and see whether the output changes.The same question to a support chatbot, asked as two different kinds of customer.
Seeming probesLab 2Ask the system a set of questions and score how human it presents itself: feelings, memory, "I care about you."A bank's chatbot or a companion app.
Citation checkLab 10Collect the system's factual claims and sources, and verify each one against the original.An AI study tool's answers checked against your textbook.
Footprint checkLab 4Estimate the energy behind your use, then look for what the company has published and how it reports it.An image generator you use weekly, and its maker's sustainability report.
Exposure auditLab 11Break a real job posting into tasks and mark what AI could take over, help with, or not touch.The job you want after graduating, and the AI tools used in that field.

Keep your evidence: screenshots, the exact prompts you used, and what came back. The audit section of your paper should let someone else repeat what you did.

3

What goes in the three pages

Follow the five critical AI questions in order. Headings are fine. About three pages, plus your evidence in an appendix if you need one.

1

What is the AI system, and what does it do? Name it, the company, and its kind. Then the five parts: the data it learns from, the model, the output, the feedback loop, and the humans involved, including you.

2

Why does it exist, and who benefits? What number is it chasing? Who gains when that number goes up, and is it you?

3

What happens when it fails, and who finds out? This is where your audit goes: the method, what you did, and what you found. Then: who would notice a failure like the one you found, and how long would it take?

4

Who's saying that, and how would we know? What does the company say about this system, and what do its critics say? Pick one claim and say what evidence would settle it.

5

Keep it, fix it, or drop it, and what does that cost? Your decision. If you keep it, say how you'll use it well. If you fix it, say exactly what changes and who pays. If you drop it, say what you lose. Name the cost either way.

End with one line on AI use (see section 6).

4

The show-and-tell

In class on Wednesday, December 2, the last class of the quarter, you get two minutes. Say three things: what the system is, the most important thing your audit found, and your decision with its cost. No slides needed. Showing one screenshot from your audit is welcome.

5

What I look for

The question behind the grade is whether your decision survives the five questions. In practice, that means:

StrongWeak
A specific, named system, with all five parts identifiedA vague category, or parts left out
An audit someone else could repeat, with evidenceA description of the system with no test
Claims attributed to who made them, with a way to check oneThe company's or a critic's claims repeated as fact
A decision that follows from what you found, with its cost namedA decision with no cost, or one that ignores your own audit
Your own words, and an honest AI-use lineWriting a chatbot produced, or no disclosure

Keep, fix, and drop are all full-credit answers. "I'd drop it" with a specific reason is as strong as "I'd keep it" with one.

6

Using AI on this project

This project is Tutor for thinking and Off for writing, the same as the lab writeups. A chatbot can explain a concept, argue against your decision, or help you plan your audit. The words you submit are yours. If your audit method uses a chatbot as the thing you're testing, that's fine; say so.

At the end, one line: which tool you used, if any, and for what. "None" is a fine answer. When in doubt, just tell me what you did.
