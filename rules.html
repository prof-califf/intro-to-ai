The Rules

Seven lines, borrowed from a punk club in Berkeley that has kept a room full of strangers arguing for forty years without anyone getting hurt. This class argues on purpose. These are the rules by the door.

Borrowed from 924 Gilman Street · translated for a classroom

NO RACISM
NO SEXISM
NO HOMOPHOBIA
NO TRANSPHOBIA
NO FIGHTING
NO STAGEDIVING
NO MAJOR LABEL BANDS
1

Where the rules come from

924 Gilman Street is a punk club in Berkeley, California, run as a volunteer collective since 1986. Green Day played their early shows there. The club has a hand-painted sign of rules by the door; each one is four words or fewer, and nobody has to interpret them. The rules can only be changed by vote at the members' meeting, and they've mostly stayed the same for forty years.

This class argues, on purpose, about jobs, politics, war, medicine, and whether the machines are coming for us. That kind of room needs rules. These are Gilman's, with three lines translated for a classroom.

2

What each line means here

NO RACISM · NO SEXISM · NO HOMOPHOBIA · NO TRANSPHOBIA

The sign, verbatim. These are also the university's rules (see the syllabus, section 14), but they're on this sign because, well, respect others.

NO FIGHTING

This means no attacking someone's position without evidence. "That's wrong" is fighting. "That doesn't make sense because" is pointing out something that could help someone see another angle. The point of the course is to really demystify AI, and to be able to discuss it in real-life terms.

NO STAGEDIVING

We can think of this one as no sudden lunging at each other. This means no sudden interrupting, piling on, or ambushing the person in the room who may be holding a position. Let people speak.

NO MAJOR LABEL BANDS

In the late 1980s and 1990s it was unthinkable to take your music to a major label. That meant selling out. We will interpret this Gilman Street rule as no blind love or hate for an AI company because you like or hate its product. If you like Claude, that's great. If you hate OpenAI, that's great, too. But you need to be able to think critically and talk about why. Liking what an AI system can do is not evidence about it. Neither is hating one. This class is not sponsored by an AI company. It is not my goal to influence whether you like or hate AI. My goal is to discuss and critically think about what AI actually means in different contexts.

3

Adding to the rules

These aren't carved. If something comes up that the rules don't cover, or a rule needs adjusting for how this room actually works, let me know, in class or by email, and we'll add to it or adjust it. Anything added goes on this page.
